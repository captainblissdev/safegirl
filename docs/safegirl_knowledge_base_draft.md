## Contraception

**KB-C1** — What contraception options exist for someone my age?
Common methods include condoms, the contraceptive pill, injectables, and implants. A
youth-friendly clinic can help explain what fits your situation and health history.

**KB-C2** — Can I access contraception without my parents' permission?
Kenyan health policy generally supports confidential access to reproductive health services
for adolescents, though how this works in practice can vary by facility. A youth-friendly
clinic can explain what applies where you are.

**KB-C3** — What should I do if a condom breaks?
Emergency contraception can reduce the chance of pregnancy if taken within a few days.
Visiting a clinic soon after is also a good idea to check for possible STI exposure.

**KB-C4** — How effective is the contraceptive pill?
When taken correctly and consistently, the pill is highly effective, but missing doses
lowers its effectiveness. A provider can help you find a method that fits your routine.

**KB-C5** — Where can I get emergency contraception?
Youth-friendly clinics and many pharmacies provide emergency contraception. It works best
the sooner it's taken after unprotected sex.

## STIs

**KB-S1** — How do I know if I have an STI?
Some STIs cause symptoms like unusual discharge, pain, or sores, but many have no symptoms
at all. Testing is the only reliable way to know for sure.

**KB-S2** — Can I get tested for STIs confidentially?
Yes — youth-friendly clinics offer confidential testing, meaning your results and visit
aren't shared without your consent.

**KB-S3** — Can you get an STI even if you used protection?
Condoms greatly lower the risk but don't remove it completely. Regular testing is still
worthwhile if you're sexually active.

**KB-S4** — How long after possible exposure should I get tested?
This depends on the specific infection — some show up on tests sooner than others. A
clinic can advise on timing for a specific concern.

**KB-S5** — Can STIs be cured?
Some STIs (like many bacterial infections) can be fully treated with medication. Others
require ongoing management. A healthcare provider can explain what applies to a specific
diagnosis.

## Pregnancy

**KB-P1** — How do I know if I'm pregnant?
Common early signs include a missed period, nausea, and fatigue — but a pregnancy test is
the only way to confirm it.

**KB-P2** — I think I might be pregnant. What should I do?
It can help to take a pregnancy test as soon as possible and speak with a healthcare
provider — a youth-friendly clinic can talk through your options without judgment.

**KB-P3** — Is it safe to visit a clinic if I'm under 18 and pregnant?
Yes — youth-friendly clinics are set up to support adolescents through pregnancy
confidentially and without judgment.

**KB-P4** — What happens at a first antenatal visit?
It typically includes a health check, some basic tests, and a conversation about your
pregnancy and any concerns you have. It's a good first step regardless of your plans going
forward.

**KB-P5** — Can I continue school if I'm pregnant?
Kenyan policy generally supports continued education for pregnant students, though
practical arrangements vary by school. A trusted adult or counselor can help navigate this.

## General

**KB-GEN1** — How does this app protect my privacy?
No phone number or name is ever collected. Your session isn't linked to your identity, and
nothing you send can be traced back to you.

**KB-GEN2** — Does this app work without internet?
Some content is available offline once loaded, but sending new questions requires a
connection.

**KB-GEN3** — Can I talk to a real person instead of the chat?
Yes — the app can connect you to real support resources and youth-friendly clinics when
needed.
